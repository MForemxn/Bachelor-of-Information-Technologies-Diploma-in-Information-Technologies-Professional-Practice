# Email [[Security]] and Social Engineering Attack
Email [[security]] is crucial in protecting sensitive information from unauthorised access and cyber threats. Social engineering attacks exploit human psychology to manipulate individuals into divulging confidential information. Understanding these threats and implementing robust [[security]] measures can significantly reduce the risk of [[data]] breaches.

## Social Engineering Attack Cycle
The social engineering attack cycle involves several stages, including research, hook, play, and exit. Attackers gather information, establish trust, exploit the target, and then disengage without detection.

- Research: Gathering information about the target.
- Hook: Establishing trust with the target.
- Play: Exploiting the target to gain information.
- Exit: Disengaging without raising suspicion.

## Social Engineering Attack Tactics
Attackers use various tactics to manipulate individuals, such as phishing, pretexting, baiting, and tailgating. These tactics exploit human vulnerabilities to gain access to sensitive information.

- Phishing: Sending fraudulent emails to obtain sensitive [[data]].
- Pretexting: Creating a fabricated scenario to extract information.
- Baiting: Offering something enticing to lure victims.
- Tailgating: Following someone into a restricted area.

## PGP (Pretty Good Privacy)
PGP is an [[encryption]] program that provides cryptographic privacy and [[authentication]] for email communication. It uses a combination of symmetric and asymmetric [[encryption]] to secure messages.

- Encrypts emails for [[confidentiality]].
- Uses digital signatures for [[authentication]].
- Combines symmetric and asymmetric [[encryption]].

## S/MIME
S/MIME (Secure/Multipurpose Internet Mail Extensions) is a standard for public key [[encryption]] and signing of MIME [[data]]. It enhances email [[security]] by providing [[encryption]], [[authentication]], and message [[integrity]].

- Encrypts email content for privacy.
- Provides digital signatures for authenticity.
- Ensures message [[integrity]].

## [[DKIM]]
[[DKIM]] (DomainKeys Identified Mail) is an email [[authentication]] method that allows the receiver to check if an email was sent and authorised by the owner of that domain. It uses digital signatures to verify the sender's identity.

- Verifies sender's domain identity.
- Uses digital signatures for [[authentication]].
- Helps prevent email spoofing.

## Sender Policy Framework ([[SPF]])
[[SPF]] is an email validation system designed to prevent spam by detecting email spoofing. It allows domain owners to specify which mail servers are permitted to send emails on their behalf.

- Prevents email spoofing.
- Specifies authorised mail servers.
- Reduces spam and phishing attacks.

## DMARC
DMARC (Domain-based Message [[Authentication]], Reporting & Conformance) is an email [[authentication]] protocol that builds on [[SPF]] and [[DKIM]]. It provides a mechanism for domain owners to publish policies and receive reports about email [[authentication]] failures.

- Builds on [[SPF]] and [[DKIM]].
- Publishes policies for email [[authentication]].
- Provides reports on [[authentication]] failures.

During my role at CDM Computers, I was tasked with leading a major infrastructure upgrade for the Juniors group of clubs. This project became a formative experience in understanding what it means to operate as an IT professional. The task involved replacing 35 user devices that could no longer be upgraded to Windows 11, consolidating six servers and a NAS into a single large server with multiple virtual machines, and implementing a backup NAS. Complicating matters, the approaching end-of-life for Windows 10 devices imposed a strict six-week deadline. The client’s licensing and directory structure required migration to hybrid Active Directory with Entra ID synchronisation and subscription-based licensing. As the most junior member of the four-person company, I led this client almost independently, seeking guidance from colleagues before escalating to my supervisor.

The critical incident that stands out occurred during the client pitch to the CEO, CTO, and other senior staff members. While the technical plan had been internally approved, presenting it to the client required reframing highly technical solutions in terms of business outcomes. Explaining why it was prudent to undertake all upgrades simultaneously — particularly when only the Windows 10 devices were urgent for cybersecurity compliance — was challenging. I needed to clearly communicate how consolidating servers and migrating to hybrid AD would minimize downtime, reduce long-term costs, and create a more scalable IT environment. This experience highlighted that IT professionals must act as translators between technical solutions and business decision-making (Chebrolu & Ness 2013; Sadun et al. 2014). Successfully communicating these benefits required careful planning and the ability to anticipate questions, reflecting the importance of strategic communication and stakeholder engagement in IT project management (Selvakumar et al. 2024; PMI 2017).

Reflecting on this experience, I realised the value of foresight, thorough planning, and seeking multiple perspectives. Shortly after the pitch, I discovered that I had underspecified the NAS storage, which quickly filled and required a follow-up proposal. This incident reinforced the importance of avoiding tunnel vision, ensuring technical planning accounts for long-term operational requirements, and reviewing work with others. The experience also revealed a gap in my professional communication skills: while confident discussing technical matters with my internal team, presenting to executives demanded clarity, conciseness, and an emphasis on business value (Williams 2017; Deshpande 2020). This distinction between internal and external communication is a critical skill for IT professionals, particularly those involved in client-facing roles.

Despite these challenges, several aspects were highly positive. Leading the project enhanced my confidence in managing client relationships and reinforced the value of taking ownership of complex technical solutions. My research and preparation allowed me to propose a solution that was both technically sound and strategically aligned with the client’s business needs. The experience demonstrated the tangible impact of IT work on business continuity, efficiency, and risk mitigation, strengthening my commitment to pursuing a career in IT consultancy and client-facing technical roles.

Looking forward, I intend to build on these lessons. Short-term, I plan to practise simplifying complex technical concepts for non-technical stakeholders and develop visual aids and financial justifications to support business cases. Long-term, I aim to participate in more projects of this scale, refining both my technical and communication skills to become an IT professional capable of influencing strategic decisions. I now understand that technical expertise alone is insufficient; bridging the gap between technology and business requires deliberate planning, clear communication, and attention to stakeholder needs (Santos-Olmo et al. 2023; UNCTAD 2025). This experience has been pivotal in shaping my professional identity and will inform how I approach future challenges in the IT industry.

# Technical vs Transferrable Skills
- transferrable skills are harder to learn and take longer to develop
	- they are the most important from an execution POV
	- employers often place more importance on transferrable skills
# Graduate Skills Lacking
- businesses often say understanding of organisation, commercial awareness, and communication skills are the 3 biggest takeaways. 

## [[Cover Letter]] Fundamentals

### Definition and Purpose

- **Primary [[function]]**: Highlight most relevant skills and experience for specific position
- **Key opportunity**: Express genuine motivation for the role
- **Critical rule**: Do NOT reiterate [[resume]] content

### Application Context

- **Professional internships**: [[Cover letter]] required
- **Casual positions**: May not require [[cover letter]]

## [[Cover Letter]] Strategy

### Core Approach

- Frame letter around how your skills/experience [[help]] the employer
- Avoid mentioning how the role benefits you personally
- Use selection criteria as your guide for content structure

### Characteristics of Outstanding Cover Letters

- **Well-written**: Tells engaging, coherent story
- **Relevant**: Matches reader's key interests and requirements
- **Unique**: Distinguishes you from other candidates
- **Substantiated**: Provides convincing evidence of claims
- **Visually pleasing**: Professional formatting and presentation

## [[Cover Letter]] Structure and Format

### Technical Specifications

- **Length**: One page maximum (experience dependent)
- **Specificity**: Tailored to individual job posting
- **Focus**: Address specific selection criteria from job advertisement

### Essential Sections

1. **Polite Address**: "Dear [Name]" (research contact name when possible, otherwise "Dear Sir or Madam")
2. **Reason for Writing**: [[State]] specific position and where you found the advertisement
3. **Job Fit Summary**: Brief explanation of why role suits you
4. **Addressing Criteria**: 150-350 words per selection criterion
5. **Professional Close**: Forward-looking statement (e.g., "I look forward to...")

## [[Resume]] Considerations

### Key Principles

- Professional formatting essential
- Content should complement, not duplicate [[cover letter]]
- Focus on achievements and quantifiable results
- Tailor content to match job requirements

## Skills Framework for Applications

### [[Technical vs Transferable Skills]]

- **Technical Skills**: Role-specific abilities that don't easily transfer
- **Transferable Skills**: Portable skills applicable across roles (communication, [[time management]])

### Industry Priorities

- Employers prioritize transferable skills over technical skills for graduates
- Cultural fit to organization is crucial
- Interpersonal and communication skills most valued
- Academic results and work experience secondary to soft skills

### Communication Skills in IT/Engineering

Multiple communication modes required:

- **Written**: Tender responses, business cases, emails, user manuals, code commenting
- **Verbal**: Interviews, meetings, presentations, stand-ups, [[feedback]] sessions
- **Visual**: Technical drawings, graphs, PowerPoint, [[process]] mapping, prototypes
- **Non-verbal**: Body language, video calls

## Application [[Process]] Strategy

### Job Selection

- Target positions you genuinely qualify for
- Prioritize roles with clear selection criteria
- Screenshot job advertisements to preserve details

### Documentation Requirements

- Tailored [[resume]] addressing job requirements
- [[Cover letter]] specifically addressing selection criteria
- Copy of original job advertisement for reference
